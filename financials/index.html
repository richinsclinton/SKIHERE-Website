The plan at a glance

Transparent. Conservative.

Ticket prices are held flat for ten years while costs rise 3% a year. The estimate carries a $17.9M contingency. Full tables are in the PDF.

Built for bad winters
Resilient.

Stress-tested against a 25% drop in visits. A pre-sold season-pass base and a capital reserve keep the resort paying its bond.

The ask in context

A one-time investment.

Not a tax increase. The $60M state investment is a one-time cost paid from existing revenue. In the 2024-25 season alone, skiing generated $342.6M in state and local tax. The state share is less than one season of that, paid once.

Not a first for Utah

The state already builds places to play.

Utah funds recreation at this scale regularly. The $60M state share here matches what the Legislature approved for the Bear Lake marina alone.

Parks and recreation, 2021 and 2022
$185M

Approved by the Legislature for 31 state park, trail and recreation projects in two sessions. More than the full $104.19M cost of this project.1
