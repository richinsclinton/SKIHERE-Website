Why now
Average age of a Utah skier
40→48

In seven seasons (2017–18 to 2024–25). The sport is aging out. Kids who can't afford a lift ticket don't grow up to be skiers.

The Games come home
2034

In 2002, Utahns watched the athletes and saw themselves, because they skied too. In 2034, we'll show off world-class skiing to a generation that can't afford to join in. Unless we change that.

The proposal

A mountain for the rest of us.

A resident-first public ski area, built for the people who live here. A community mountain with real Wasatch terrain at prices Utah families can actually pay.

Precedent

Not radical. Proven.

Public ski areas have worked for decades. And Utah already runs resident-first golf and hunting. Same idea, better snow.

United States
Other states already do this.
Several states run public ski areas the way Utah runs public golf: resident passes, below-market day tickets.
They're justified by community access and regional impact, not standalone profit.
Europe · Canada · Scandinavia · Japan
So does the rest of the world.
Towns across Europe, Canada, Scandinavia and Japan have run ski hills as public utilities for generations.
If Austria, France and Japan can do it, the state with the Greatest Snow on Earth and the 2034 Games can lead.
2013

Utah opened the nation's first state Office of Outdoor Recreation. 24 states followed. A public ski area puts Utah out front again.

The first step

Step one isn't a chairlift. It's a study.

We're not asking Utah to build anything yet. We're asking for an independent feasibility study: where it could go, what it would cost, how it would run. Your signature is how we get it funded.
